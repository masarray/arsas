Open-source reliability

Trust the exact release because its evidence can be inspected.

ARSAS does not ask you to trust a logo or a black box. Source, package identity, automated regression evidence and field boundaries remain independently reviewable.

01

Inspect the source

The ARSAS application, ARIEC61850 engine, commit history and public issue trail are visible. Open source means the implementation can be reviewed—not that correctness is assumed.

03

Make changes prove themselves

Application regression tests, Smart Discovery production-route guards, website/evidence validators and release checks run in CI. They catch regressions inside their declared scope; they do not simulate every physical relay.

Inspect CI history →
04

Keep field claims bounded

Compatibility evidence is published per service and condition. Known limitations, negative outcomes and cases requiring physical-relay validation stay visible instead of being converted into broad compatibility claims.

Review field evidence →
What this does not mean:Open source is not automatic correctness. CI is not FAT/SAT acceptance. A supply-chain attestation is not a reproducible-build claim. ARSAS exposes enough evidence for engineers to review the exact release and decide what still requires independent field verification.
